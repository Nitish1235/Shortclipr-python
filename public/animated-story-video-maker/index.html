THE SHORT STORY SPINE

Want.
Obstacle. Change.

A short animated story is not a smaller version of a feature film. It works when one change carries the whole meaning.

  1. Want: give a character one understandable goal.
  2. Obstacle: introduce one thing that makes the goal difficult.
  3. Choice: make the character do something in response.
  4. Change: show what is different at the end because of that choice.

Keep the world, cast, and message small enough to fit the time available.

WORKED EXAMPLE

One small fear.
One brave choice.

Story idea: a paper boat is afraid of rain.

Example brief

“Create a 30-second animated story about a paper boat that fears the rain because it thinks water will destroy it. A sudden shower leaves it stuck near a puddle. The boat chooses to float into the puddle and discovers it carries it toward home. End with the boat moving confidently through the water.”

The brief gives the character a fear, a visible obstacle, a choice, and an ending that changes the original situation. Keep the final video focused on that one movement rather than adding extra plot.

How many scenes fit in a 60-second animated story?+

Aim for 5 to 7 scenes—enough room for setup, obstacle, turning point, and a clean emotional payoff without rushing.

How do I keep a character looking consistent?+

Describe the character's signature color, outfit, and key prop consistently in your brief, and review every cut for continuity.

Yes. Mini fables, character comedy, and one-clue mystery shorts are core story formats.
