FORMAT STARTERS

IDEAS, IN MOTION.

Pick one format that gives your short a clear beginning, middle, and ending. Do not try to combine every idea into one video.

HOW TO PICK AN IDEA

Start with the
viewer’s job.

Ask what the viewer should do by the end of the short. If they should understand something, make an explainer. If they should notice a difference, make a comparison. If they should make a prediction, make a quiz. If they should care about a change, make a story.

Then decide what can be shown. A short gets stronger when the prompt describes visible action, not only a topic.

01

Animated explainers

Best for: one question with a visual answer.
Prompt start: “Explain why [question] to [audience]. Show [relationship] and end with [takeaway].”

02

Comparisons and visual facts

Best for: making a difference visible.
Prompt start: “Compare [A] and [B] at the same scale. Show [meaningful difference] and explain why it matters.”

03

Quizzes and visual puzzles

Best for: invite a fair guess, then teach.
Prompt start: “Ask [question], show [choices], leave time to think, reveal [answer], and explain why.”

04

Character comedy

Best for: one simple conflict and comic payoff.
Prompt start: “A [character] wants [goal], but [obstacle] keeps getting in the way. End with [twist].”

05

Mini stories and fables

Best for: a small meaningful change.
Prompt start: “A [character] wants [goal], chooses to [action] after [obstacle], and ends by [change].”

06

Mystery and suspense

Best for: a clue, question, and reveal.
Prompt start: “Show [unusual clue], let the viewer wonder why it happened, then reveal [answer] in the ending.”

07

Horror shorts

Best for: a small unsettling detail, not graphic shock.
Prompt start: “A familiar place changes when [detail]. Build tension through [visual], then end with [reveal].”

08

Historical stories

Best for: one event, person, route, or decision.
Prompt start: “Show how [event] unfolded through [sequence]. Explain [context] and verify all historical details.”

09

Maps and geography

Best for: routes, place, distance, and change.
Prompt start: “Follow [route/place] from [start] to [end]. Show [change] and explain what viewers should notice.”

10

Space and scale journeys

Best for: size, distance, and perspective.
Prompt start: “Compare [thing] with [reference] to show its scale. Keep the figures and labels accurate.”

11

What-if simulations

Best for: a clearly stated hypothetical.
Prompt start: “What if [scenario]? State the assumption, show likely effects, and distinguish illustration from certainty.”

12

Myth-busting experiments

Best for: examine a claim with evidence.
Prompt start: “Test the claim that [claim]. Show [evidence], explain the result, and cite or verify the source.”

13

Animated conversations

Best for: two understandable perspectives.
Prompt start: “Let [character A] and [character B] explore [question]. Give each a clear point and end with [resolution].”

14

Sports tactics

Best for: movement and decisions.
Prompt start: “Show how [tactic] works in [sport]. Mark the key movement and explain the decision it creates.”

15

Architecture and room transformations

Best for: before-and-after changes in space.
Prompt start: “Show [space] before and after [change]. Make the layout, function, and visual difference clear.”

16

News and event recaps

Best for: verified context around a current event.
Prompt start: “Recap [event] using verified facts, clear dates, context, and sources. Avoid unverified claims.”

MAKE THE PROMPT SPECIFIC

One format.
One focused brief.

Before creating, add five decisions to any prompt: who is watching, what they should understand or feel, what must be visible, what tone fits, and what changes by the ending.

For factual, historical, health, legal, financial, or current-event subjects, verify the final copy and visuals before publishing. ShortClipr can create the animated short; it does not replace source review or editorial judgement.
